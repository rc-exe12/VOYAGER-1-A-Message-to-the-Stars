The one light-day milestone

One light-day is the distance light travels in 24 hours: 299,792.458 km/s × 86,400 s ≈ 25.9 billion km (≈173 AU). NASA’s stated crossing was 18 November 2026 at 10:16:07 UTC, so a radio signal now needs a full day to reach Voyager 1 — and another day for the reply. Signal travel time is calculated; communication status must come from NASA.

Operating in interstellar space

NASA has been switching off instruments one by one to stretch the spacecraft’s shrinking power from its radioisotope generators, which lose roughly 4 watts a year. Which instruments are still on, and the latest engineering updates, change over time — see the official Voyager 1 page rather than relying on this summary. Communication runs through NASA’s Deep Space Network at a very low data rate.

Timeline

1977Launch

5 September 1977, Cape Canaveral. The start of a “grand tour” mission that was only meant to last a few years.

1979Jupiter encounter

Closest approach 5 March 1979. Voyager 1 revealed volcanic activity on the moon Io — the first active volcanoes seen beyond Earth — and detailed Jupiter’s storms and thin rings.

1980Saturn encounter

Flyby on 12 November 1980 studied Saturn’s rings and the thick atmosphere of Titan. Titan’s gravity flung the spacecraft out of the planetary plane, ending its planetary tour.

1990Pale Blue Dot

On 14 February 1990 Voyager 1 turned its camera back and photographed Earth from about 6 billion km away.

2012Interstellar space

In August 2012 Voyager 1 crossed the heliopause into interstellar space — the first human-made object to do so. NASA confirmed it in 2013.

2026One light-day

On 18 November 2026 at 10:16:07 UTC (per NASA’s published prediction) Voyager 1 reached 25.9 billion km from Earth: the first spacecraft a full light-day away. Voyager 2 is expected to follow around 2035.

Pale Blue Dot

Earth

Carl Sagan urged NASA to point the camera home before it was switched off. From roughly 6 billion km (about 40 AU) Earth covered less than a pixel, caught in a streak of scattered sunlight. Sagan saw in it the case for caring for the only home we have. This is a schematic — view the authentic NASA photograph at NASA Science (image credit: NASA/JPL-Caltech).

The Golden Record

A gift from a small planet.

Each Voyager carries a 12-inch gold-plated copper phonograph record with instructions for playing it, made to represent life and culture on Earth. A NASA committee chaired by Carl Sagan selected its contents; Ann Druyan, Frank Drake, Jon Lomberg and Timothy Ferris were among its creators.

Our Message

YOU’VE DONE GREAT, VOYAGER.

Dear Voyager 1,

You started your journey when the world was very different.

You travelled past giant planets, crossed the boundary of our solar system and ventured into the unknown.

You carry the sounds, images and greetings of a tiny planet called Earth.

You remind us how far human curiosity can travel.

We don’t know how long your journey will continue, but we want you to know that you’ve already made history.

Thank you for everything.

Keep going. Keep travelling.

BEST OF LUCK, VOYAGER 1.

With love, from Earth. ❤️
